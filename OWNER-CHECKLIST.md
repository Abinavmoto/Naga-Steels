## 7. Two things that will bring you more business than the website

Being honest about where the results actually come from.

### Your Google listing matters more than this website
When someone searches "chain link fencing Madurai", Google shows a **map box with three
businesses at the very top**, above all normal results. Getting into that box is decided
almost entirely by your Google Business Profile, not by your website.

- [ ] **Add photos** of your godown, the stock, the shopfront and finished jobs. Google
      strongly favours listings with real, recent photos.
- [ ] **Ask customers for reviews.** After a delivery goes well, send the review link on
      WhatsApp. Ten reviews will do more for your phone ringing than anything on this site.

### Real photos of your yard, the single highest-value thing you can send me
Every photograph on the site is currently a **conceptual application visual**, and each
one carries a visible caption saying so. Nothing claims to be your work when it is not.

They are relevant pictures, but they are not **yours**. In this trade a photo of actual
stock in an actual godown converts better than any design work, and it is the one thing a
competitor cannot copy.

If you can send me:
- [ ] The shopfront / signboard
- [ ] Stock in the godown: rolls of chain link, sheets stacked, wire coils
- [ ] Two or three completed fencing jobs
- [ ] A photo of you at the shop

...I will replace the conceptual images and remove the disclaimers.

**The homepage now needs 24 photographs.** The new layout is built around real
photography, and every slot currently holds a labelled grey placeholder. Nothing is
broken, but the page will not look finished until they are replaced. The full list,
with exact sizes, is in `CONTENT-SOURCES.md` under "Homepage image slot manifest".
The three that matter most, in order:

1. **Three tall hero photographs** (portrait, 2:3). One fencing, one steel/sheet, one
   site or loaded lorry. These are the first thing anyone sees.
2. **Fifteen square product photographs** (1:1), each a single product on a plain white
   background — a roll of chain link, a wire coil, a sheet stack, a box of nails. Phone
   photos against a white wall are fine. These feed the scrolling product strip.
3. **Three wider shots** (landscape) for the news strip: yard stock, a chain-link roll,
   a loaded vehicle.

If photographs are slow to arrange, send the fifteen square product shots first. They
are the easiest to take and they fill the most visible gap.
