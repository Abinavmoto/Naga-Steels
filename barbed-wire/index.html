The trap: barbed wire is sold by weight

This is the one thing to understand before comparing quotes, and it catches out experienced buyers. Barbed wire is priced per kilogram, but what you actually need is length. Those two are only related through the gauge and the barb spacing.

A coil with barbs every 6 inches contains fewer barbs and less twisted wire per kilogram than one with barbs every 3 inches, so it gives you more running length for the same money. Likewise a lighter gauge stretches further per kilogram.

The result is that the cheapest rate per kilogram can easily be the most expensive fence per running foot. Always ask for three things together: the gauge, the barb spacing, and the approximate running length per coil. Then you are comparing fences rather than comparing scrap metal.

How much do you need?

For a standalone barbed wire boundary, the usual build is five to seven strands on posts at 8–10 foot centres. So multiply your perimeter by the number of strands to get the total running length, then add roughly 10% for tying off, corner wraps and sag.

As a worked example: a 1-acre square plot has a perimeter of about 835 feet. At six strands that is roughly 5,000 running feet of wire, plus about 90 posts at 10 foot centres, plus struts at the corners and the gate.

For a three-strand topping above a chain link fence, it is simply three times the perimeter. The material calculator will give you the post count for either arrangement.

Barbed wire or chain link?

They solve different problems, and the honest answer depends on what the boundary is for.

  • Barbed wire is far cheaper per running foot and goes up fast over long distances. It marks a boundary and deters livestock, but it does not stop a determined person and it will not keep small animals in or out.
  • Chain link costs several times more per foot but gives a real physical barrier, contains animals properly, and looks like a finished boundary rather than a field edge.

A common and sensible compromise on farm properties: chain link along the roadside and the gate approach where it is seen and where security matters, barbed wire on the remaining field boundaries. We supply both, so you can split the order across one bill.

Concertina and razor coil

For industrial perimeter, godowns and substations, we also supply concertina razor coil in flat-blade or cross-blade, in standard coil diameters. It is normally installed above a chain link fence rather than on its own, and it should be handled with proper gloves. Tell us the perimeter length and whether you want it wall-mounted or on a fence top.

Fixings

Barbed wire needs straining at the ends and stapling or tying along the line. We stock the supporting material: GI tension and tie wire, turnbuckles, straining bolts and fencing staples, see nails, ropes and fasteners. Ordering it together means one delivery instead of a second trip when you find you are short of staples.
