Choosing the gauge

Chain link is sold by Standard Wire Gauge (SWG), and the gauge is the single biggest lever on both price and service life. The numbering runs backwards, a lower SWG number means thicker wire.

The step between gauges is larger than most buyers expect. Going from 10 SWG to 12 SWG drops the wire from 3.25 mm to 2.64 mm, a reduction of roughly a third of the steel in every foot of fence. That is exactly why one quote can look dramatically cheaper than another for what appears to be the same product. Check the gauge before you compare the rate.

  • 8 SWG (4.06 mm) for industrial perimeter, solar parks, substations, godown boundaries. Anywhere the fence is a security measure rather than a marker.
  • 10 SWG (3.25 mm) is the standard for agricultural boundary and general site fencing. Handles cattle pressure. This is what most buyers actually want.
  • 12 SWG (2.64 mm) for garden boundaries, poultry runs, light screening and temporary enclosures where nothing will lean on it.

Choosing the aperture

The aperture is the size of the diamond opening. A tighter aperture puts more wire into the same area, so it costs more per running foot but gives a stronger, less climbable fence. A wider aperture is the economical choice over long distances.

Hot-dip galvanized, PVC coated or electro-galvanized

The coating decides how long the fence survives, and the right answer depends more on where you are than on what you are fencing.

Hot-dip galvanized (GI)

The wire is drawn through molten zinc, so the coating metallurgically bonds to the steel and covers every cut end and knuckle. Nothing is left bare to start rusting. Expect 15–25 years in inland Tamil Nadu conditions at a heavy coating grade. This is the default recommendation for most work.

PVC coated

A galvanized core sealed inside a plastic jacket, usually green. It lasts 20–30 years and performs distinctly better in humid and salt-laden air. If your site is within about 20 km of the coast (Ramanathapuram, Rameswaram, Tuticorin) PVC costs more per foot but is almost always the cheaper fence over its life. It also disappears visually into planting, which matters for residential work.

Electro-galvanized

A thin, bright, very even zinc layer applied electrically. It looks the best of the three on day one and lasts the shortest outdoors, around 5–8 years. Correct for indoor partitions, machine guarding and temporary site barricades. Not a boundary fence material.

Zinc coating weight, the number nobody quotes

Two rolls of hot-dip galvanized chain link at the same gauge can have wildly different lifespans, because "galvanized" says nothing about how much zinc. That is measured in GSM (grams per square metre) under IS 4826, and it typically ranges from 40 to 275+.

A 40 GSM commercial coating and a 275 GSM heavy coating are visually identical when they are installed. Four years later they are not. If a quote does not state the GSM, it is not a complete quote, ask for it in writing. We will always tell you the grade we are supplying.

Working out how much you need

Chain link is priced by weight but bought by running feet, which is where confusion usually starts. The weight for a given length depends on the gauge and the aperture together, so two quotes are only comparable when both match.

As a starting point: measure the perimeter, pick the height, and allow one line post every 10 feet plus two struts at every corner and gate. The material calculator on the home page does this arithmetic and tells you the number of rolls, posts and the total mesh area.
