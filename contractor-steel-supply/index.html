Application · 06

Materials for contractors and fabricators.

Specification-led material enquiries for fabrication, fencing and project execution, quoted against a schedule rather than item by item over the phone.

Scope Material supply

Six product families on one invoice. Civil work, erection and installation are a separate scope.

The requirement

A contractor's problem is almost never finding one material. It is getting eleven line items from four suppliers onto one site in the same week without three of them being wrong.

Six product families from one yard is the practical answer to that: one quotation, one invoice, and where quantities allow, one vehicle.

How to buy

  1. 01

    Send the BOQ. We will quote line by line against the schedule as written.

  2. 02

    Send the cutting list. Sheet priced to your pieces rather than to the full sheet.

  3. 03

    Phased release. Material dispatched against the programme, not the order date.

  4. 04

    One invoice. Fencing, sheet, wire, mesh, roofing and fixings together.

  5. 05

    Freight separated. Transport quoted apart from material, always.

Working together

What makes a quote useful.

Drawings, bills of quantity, reference photographs and line-item schedules all help turn a general request into a usable quotation. Without them, both sides end up estimating, and estimates are where margin and goodwill go.

Where a specification will cost more than the job needs, we will say so before quoting. That is usually a gauge heavier than required, or a coating grade the location does not justify. It is a smaller invoice and a better relationship.

We are a material supplier and a fencing manufacturer. Civil work, erection and installation are a separate scope, and we will not quote for them rather than subcontract a trade we do not control.
