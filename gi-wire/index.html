Gauge selection by job

GI wire is one product across a very wide range of uses, and the only thing that distinguishes them is the gauge and the coating. Order the wrong gauge and it either snaps in your hands or refuses to twist.

Rebar binding wire

18 SWG (1.22 mm) annealed or galvanized wire is the standard for tying reinforcement on site. The critical property is not strength, it is ductility. The wire has to twist tight around a bar junction and stay twisted without work-hardening and snapping at the third turn.

This is why over-specifying here backfires. A harder, heavier wire slows a bar bender down, wastes material on broken ties, and ends up costing more in labour than it saved in steel. If your team is complaining that the binding wire keeps breaking, the gauge or the temper is wrong.

As a rough planning figure, allow around 8 to 12 kg of binding wire per tonne of reinforcement steel, depending on bar spacing and how tightly the job is detailed.

Fence tension line wire

A chain link fence is only as taut as its line wire. Tension wire runs horizontally along the top, bottom and often the middle of the run, and the chain link fabric is tied to it. Without it, the fabric sags between posts within a season and never recovers.

Use 10 SWG for the tension lines and 16 SWG for the ties that attach the fabric. As a rule of thumb, allow roughly 0.06 kg of tie wire per running foot of fence at standard tie spacing, the material calculator works this out alongside the roll and post count.

Zinc coating grades

The same reasoning applies here as to chain link: the word "galvanized" tells you nothing on its own. The coating weight in GSM under IS 4826 is what determines how long the wire survives.

  • Commercial (40–60 GSM), indoor use, binding wire that will be buried in concrete within days, temporary work. Anything encased in concrete does not need a heavy coating, because the concrete protects it.
  • Medium (80–120 GSM), general outdoor tying, residential fencing, garden and trellis work.
  • Heavy (200–275 GSM), fence tension lines, farm boundaries, anything exposed for years. Worth the premium because replacing a failed tension line means taking the whole fence down.

Near the coast, Ramanathapuram, Rameswaram, Tuticorin, step up one grade from these recommendations, or use PVC coated wire.

Annealed or galvanized?

A distinction worth getting right, because they are not interchangeable:

Black annealed wire is heat-softened, very ductile and uncoated. It ties beautifully and is the cheaper option, but it will rust in weeks if it is left exposed. It is the correct choice for rebar binding precisely because the concrete goes over it.

Galvanized wire is stiffer to work with but survives outdoors. Use it for anything that will remain visible or exposed once the job is finished.
