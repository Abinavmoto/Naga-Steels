Delivery · South Tamil Nadu

Dispatched from Madurai, across the southern districts

We hold stock at Chitrakkara Street rather than ordering it in when you ask, which is why collection is usually same-day. Below are the towns we run to regularly, with honest lead times and what the lorry minimum actually means for you.

How freight is quoted

We quote the material and the freight as two separate figures, never rolled together into one rate. This is deliberate. A bundled rate hides the transport cost, and hidden transport cost is where a cheap-looking quote turns expensive.

Seeing the two separately means you can make an informed decision: send your own vehicle, collect it yourself, or wait and combine with a larger order. Sometimes the honest answer is that you should not be paying us to deliver at all, and we would rather say so than take the freight.

The lorry minimum, explained properly

For delivery outside Madurai city, a load is typically 500 kg or more. That is not an arbitrary rule to push up order sizes, it is the point below which the freight per kilogram starts climbing steeply enough that you are better off collecting.

If your order is smaller than that, we will tell you. The usual options are to collect from the godown, to combine it with a neighbour's order if you are fencing adjoining plots, or to wait for the next run in your direction. All three are cheaper for you than a part-loaded lorry.

Long roofing sheets need planning

Roofing sheets cut to a long rafter length need a suitable vehicle and careful loading. A kinked sheet is a scrapped sheet, and it is not a problem you can fix on site.

So tell us the cut length when you enquire, not when you confirm. It changes which vehicle we send and it changes the freight. Discovering a 20-foot sheet at loading time means a delay for everyone.

Coastal districts, specify differently

For sites in Ramanathapuram, and generally anywhere within about 20 km of the coast, salt-laden air shortens the life of galvanized steel considerably. Our standing recommendation for those locations:

  • Step up one zinc coating grade from what you would use inland, or move to PVC coated fabric entirely
  • Use galvalume rather than plain GI for roofing
  • Use galvanized fasteners without exception, a plain bolt in coastal air will fail long before the fence does

It costs more up front. Over the life of the fence it is consistently the cheaper decision, and we would rather quote it correctly the first time than replace it in five years.
