Wire Nails, Steel Rope & GI Bolts

Mild steel wire nails by size and weight, galvanized and ungalvanized steel wire rope for rigging and lifting, and the GI fasteners and fencing fittings that finish the job.

Mild steel wire nails

Nails are sold by weight, not by count, and specified by length × gauge. Ordering "2 inch nails" without the gauge leaves the choice to whoever picks the box, and a 2 inch nail in a heavy gauge will split a rafter that a lighter gauge would have held perfectly.

Wire nail sizes and applications
LengthMetricTypical use
½" – 1"12 – 25 mmBeading, ply lining, panel fixing, light joinery
1½" – 2"38 – 50 mmGeneral carpentry, batten fixing, packing cases
2½" – 3"63 – 75 mmShuttering, formwork, door and window frames
4" – 6"100 – 150 mmHeavy timber, roof framing, structural carpentry

For shuttering and formwork the usual demand is 2½ inch and 3 inch, bought by the bag. Plain MS nails are correct here, the shuttering comes down again, so there is no reason to pay for galvanizing.

For anything that stays outdoors (fencing battens, roof framing, gates) use galvanized nails. A plain nail in an exposed joint bleeds a rust stain down the timber within one monsoon, and then fails.

Steel wire rope

Wire rope is specified by diameter and construction, and the construction matters as much as the size. It is written as two numbers: the count of strands, and the count of wires in each strand.

  • 6×19, fewer, thicker wires. Stiffer, more abrasion resistant, less flexible. Correct for standing rigging, guy lines and general hauling.
  • 6×36, more, finer wires. Considerably more flexible, so it runs over sheaves and spools onto a drum without fatiguing. Correct for crane and hoist work.

The core matters too. A fibre core (FC) rope is more flexible and cheaper; an independent wire rope core (IWRC) is stronger and resists crushing on a drum. If the rope will be spooled under load, specify IWRC.

Steel wire rope diameters and applications
DiameterTypical use
6 – 8 mmLight hauling, guy wires, tensioning, agricultural use
10 – 12 mmGeneral site rigging, winches, borewell work
14 – 16 mmCrane hoisting, heavy lifting, material handling
18 – 20 mmHeavy structural lifting, mooring, towing

On lifting: the safe working load of a rope is a fraction of its breaking load, and the correct factor depends on the application, the terminations and the condition of the rope. We supply rope to the specification you give us. We do not certify lifting assemblies, if the rope is for a crane or a hoist, have the assembly rated by a competent person. Getting this wrong injures people.

GI bolts, fittings and fencing hardware

The small items that hold a fencing job together, and the ones most often forgotten until the crew is already on site:

  • GI nuts, bolts and washers in the common metric sizes
  • Turnbuckles and straining bolts for tensioning fence line wire
  • Fencing staples for timber posts
  • U-clamps, bulldog grips and thimbles for terminating wire rope
  • Self-drilling roofing screws with EPDM washers, see roofing sheets

Buying these with the fabric and the tie wire on one bill is the difference between one delivery and a second trip into Madurai because the staples ran out at three in the afternoon.

Galvanized or plain?

A simple rule that covers almost every case: if it stays outside, galvanize it. The price difference on a bag of nails or a box of bolts is small. The cost of stripping and redoing an exposed joint that has rusted out is not.

The exception is anything that gets removed or encased, shuttering nails, or fasteners that end up inside concrete. There, plain MS is the correct and cheaper choice.
