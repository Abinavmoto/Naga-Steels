Welded mesh or chain link?

These two get confused constantly, and choosing the wrong one is an expensive mistake. The difference is structural, not cosmetic.

Chain link is woven. The wires are not joined, so the fabric can flex and be pulled taut between posts. That flexibility is why it follows uneven ground well and survives impact, it absorbs the hit and springs back.

Welded mesh is fused. Every intersection is welded, producing a rigid flat sheet that holds its square and will not sag or belly out between supports. It cannot be stretched, so it has to be supported along its length rather than tensioned.

  • Need a boundary over a long, uneven run, cheaply? Chain link.
  • Need something that stays flat, square and won't deform? Welded mesh.
  • Need the mesh buried in plaster or concrete? Welded mesh, always.

Where it gets used around Madurai

RCC plaster reinforcement

Fine welded mesh laid into wall plaster controls shrinkage cracking, particularly over the junction between brickwork and a concrete column where the two materials move at different rates. This is the single most common use we supply for, and it usually wants a light gauge in a tight grid.

Poultry and cattle enclosures

A ½ inch or 1 inch grid keeps chicks contained and keeps rats and snakes out. For cattle and goat pens, a heavier 10–12 SWG mesh in a 2–4 inch grid takes leaning and butting without deforming, which chain link at the same weight would not.

Machine guarding and factory screening

Rigid panels bolt straight to a frame and stay square, so guarding stays where it was installed. Electro-galvanized is acceptable here because it is indoors and out of the weather.

Grilles, window screens and tree guards

Panels cut to size for window and ventilator screening, gratings and tree guards. Because the sheet is rigid, it needs no frame on all four sides, two fixings are often enough.

Galvanized before or after welding

This distinction is worth understanding because it changes both the price and the life, and it is almost never volunteered in a quote.

Galvanized before welding (GBW), the wire is zinc-coated first, then welded. The weld itself burns the zinc away at each intersection, leaving small bare spots that start rusting first. Cheaper, and perfectly adequate indoors or in plaster.

Galvanized after welding (GAW), the mesh is welded first and the finished sheet is then dipped. Every joint and every cut edge is sealed. Costs more, and lasts considerably longer outdoors. For anything exposed to weather, specify GAW.

For coastal sites, PVC coated welded mesh is available and behaves the same way as PVC coated chain link, the plastic jacket carries the corrosion resistance rather than the zinc.

Ordering and cutting

Mesh is sold by area, square feet or square metres, and the weight for a given area depends on gauge and grid together. We cut rolls and panels to size in the yard, which matters more than it sounds: cutting welded mesh cleanly on site needs proper bolt cutters, and a ragged cut edge is both a safety hazard and the first place rust appears.

Tell us the finished dimensions you need and we will cut to them. For the tie wire to fix it, see GI and binding wire.
